# Research Experiment Plan

A controlled test of reusable physical representations

# 1. Decision and scope
Central question: under what training conditions does a JEPA-style video model encode a persistent physical mechanism in a form that improves prediction across new states and phenomena?
The experiment tests a bounded claim about synthetic physical prediction. It does not establish human-like concepts, general causal understanding, or a universal scaling law. A linear probe decoding gravity is supporting evidence, not the definition of abstraction.
The supplied conversation preview motivates separating mechanism usefulness from mechanism inferability. The complete referenced conversation was unavailable in this session. All numerical settings, Stage A–E definitions, thresholds, and budgets below are proposed protocol defaults, not recovered commitments. Freeze them after the pilot and before opening the confirmatory test set.
Deliverable scope: four research memos, an Excel dashboard, machine-readable contracts, CSV headers, and reference utilities. This package contains no trained models or experimental results.
# 2. Hypotheses and evidence ladder
| ID | Preregistered claim | Discriminating result |
| H1 | Histories contain useful law information beyond instantaneous state in variable, persistent worlds. | JEPA improves matched-state physical prediction over a strong state-only baseline; oracle gap is measurable. |
| H2 | Reusable abstraction benefits from both useful and inferable laws. | History benefit changes with independently measured usefulness and inferability; no unconditional monotonicity claim. |
| H3 | Persistence supports reuse. | When current law evidence is matched, longer persistence improves held-out transfer; unpredictable future switches limit both agents and non-clairvoyant oracle. |
| H4 | Capacity, data, and compute change the attainable history benefit. | Replicated effects survive explicit parameter, token, and compute controls; depth alone is not credited when these differ. |
| H5 | Representations support mechanism reuse beyond trained trajectories. | Frozen-history representation adds predictive value in held-out phenomena and cause-disambiguation tests with fixed adaptation budgets. |
Evidence ladder: (1) valid simulator and identifiable task; (2) reliable matched-state history benefit; (3) nuisance-robust and cross-phenomenon reuse; (4) controlled representation intervention. Each level requires the earlier levels. Failure at a higher level does not erase evidence at a lower one.
# 3. Synthetic 3D world and episode construction
Primary world: a sphere moving in a 3D box under uniform downward gravity, no drag, no contacts during scored free flight. Use SI units; sphere radius 0.10 m; gravity levels 4, 9, 14 m/s²; camera fixed, calibrated orthographic oblique view; RGB 96 × 96; 30 frames/s; simulation step 1/240 s. Render 64 frames per episode so the full context/horizon grid can be extracted without padding.
Choose anchor position x,y in [−1,1] m and z in [12,16] m; anchor velocity vx,vy in [−1,1] m/s, vz in [−2,2] m/s. Set evaluation anchor frame 31. Context contains c consecutive frames ending at the anchor. Score futures at offsets 4, 8, 16, or 24 frames; primary endpoint uses all frames from anchor+1 through anchor+h. Fit camera framing once to the union of supported trajectories before generating datasets.
Matched-state generation: sample one anchor state and nuisance bundle, then integrate each gravity history backward analytically and future forward analytically. At t=0 use p(t)=p0+v0 t+0.5 a t² and v(t)=v0+a t. The same anchor, appearance, camera, and lighting occur under every law. Verify state equality to 1e−6 in SI units and pixel equality of the anchor render. Every anchor-law group stays in one split.
Do not construct controls by conditioning independently generated trajectories on approximate visual similarity. Exact matched states avoid residual velocity and position confounds. Reject an entire anchor group if any law variant violates visibility/support, and report rejection by law. Freeze a shared feasible support rather than reject variants separately.
Randomize color, texture, light, and sphere material independently of law. Primary camera remains fixed to preserve identifiability; camera changes are explicit out-of-distribution tests. Log nuisance labels for leakage audits but never feed law or nuisance labels into JEPA pretraining. Use no frame-number overlays or law-dependent filenames accessible to the loader.
Bouncing and pendulum scenes are secondary phenomena. Bouncing uses fixed known restitution and floor height; score flight segments and contact outcomes separately. A pendulum uses fixed known length, constrained planar motion visible to the camera, and the same gravity regimes. Validate energy conservation and period trends before admitting these scenes. Gravity is the only hidden changing mechanism in primary experiments; secondary multi-cause tests explicitly introduce additional mechanisms.
# 4. Dataset, splits, and law regimes
Default pretraining size is 20,000 clips per dataset replica; pilot uses 5,000. Independent validation and readout-training sets each contain 3,000 clips. Locked primary test contains 500 anchor groups × 3 laws = 1,500 clips per test replica, with 5 independently generated test replicas. Create separate groups for each OOD suite. Test sample counts are proposed minima subject to pilot precision estimates.
Split by anchor family, trajectory-generation seed, and asset family before rendering. Do not split frames or law variants across partitions. Readout training is disjoint from JEPA pretraining; the encoder is frozen for the primary readout. Validation supports hyperparameters; test is opened once per frozen protocol. Test targets and law labels are unavailable to training loaders.
Nested data-size subsets use a fixed permutation within a replica; this supports paired scaling comparisons. Corpus replicas use distinct generator roots. Test sets are common across compared models and conditions within a suite, and immutable across reruns.
| Factor | Levels | Control and interpretation |
| Prediction horizon h | 4, 8, 16, 24 frames (0.133–0.8 s) | Same anchor/context and scoring units; pair horizon with context in Stage A. |
| Context length c | 2, 4, 8, 16 frames | Same sampling rate and last frame. With exact anchor velocity supplied, two noiseless positions can reveal gravity; include a one-frame pilot negative control. |
| Law diversity K | 1, 3, 5, continuous | K=1 at g=9; K=3 at 4,9,14; K=5 at 4,6.5,9,11.5,14; continuous uniform [4,14]. |
| Persistence | episode-fixed; mean dwell 32, 8, 2 frames | Geometric dwell, redraw law from prior; log actual change rate, including same-law redraws. |
| Encoder depth | 4, 8, 12 blocks | Fixed-width sweep plus separate approximately parameter-matched comparison. |
| Encoder width | 128, 256, 384 | Fix depth=8 for width sweep; heads=width/32. |
| Training data | 5k, 20k, 80k clips | Nested subsets and fixed exposure/compute comparison reported separately. |
| Training compute | 0.25×, 1×, 4× pilot-calibrated budget | Record steps, clips, tokens, FLOPs estimate, GPU-hours, hardware. |
K varies both discretization and law variance. Add a variance-matched K=3/5 comparison by rescaling centered values to a common variance within support. Report the achieved variance. K=1 is a learning-environment control, not a useful abstraction-score denominator on its own in-distribution test.
For law switches, the oracle knows the current law and true transition process but not future random draws. Compute its conditional expected trajectory by analytic integration where available or converged Monte Carlo. A clairvoyant oracle knowing future switches is an explicitly separate unattainable ceiling and is excluded from primary ARS.
# 5. JEPA implementation and comparison models
Use a small JEPA-style model trained from scratch, not a claimed replication of V-JEPA 2. A video transformer encodes only past context. Patch size 16×16, temporal patch size 1, learned spatial and temporal positions, pre-layer normalization, MLP ratio 4, no dropout by default. Mean-pool context tokens for the primary history vector z. Store per-layer pooled vectors for analysis.
A four-block, width-128 predictor receives context tokens and learned future queries indexed by target time and spatial location. A separate exponential-moving-average target encoder sees future target frames; stop gradients through targets. Predict normalized target patch embeddings with mean squared error. Future frames must never enter context attention, tokenization, normalization statistics, augmentation decisions, or caches.
Proposed optimization: AdamW, learning rate 3e−4 at batch 64, weight decay 0.05, warmup 5% of updates, cosine decay, gradient clipping at 1.0; EMA from 0.996 to 0.9999. Pilot default is 20,000 optimizer updates. Fix optimizer settings across the primary architecture comparison; allow equal-budget tuning only as a separately labeled robustness analysis.
Monitor feature variance, covariance effective rank, target norms, and constant-predictor loss. EMA alone is not assumed to prevent collapse. If collapse occurs, stop; a variance regularizer or changed mask design defines a new version of the objective and requires rerunning affected comparisons. Do not quietly repair only failed conditions.
Primary behavioral task is privileged-state-assisted: predict physical future positions given exact anchor state s, horizon h, and optionally frozen history vector z. This isolates incremental history information from perception quality. Train equal-budget MLP readouts on (s,h), (s,h,z), and (s,h,g) for learned state, JEPA, and learned-law models. Match readout hidden width/depth and training budget; use a dimension-matched random-vector control for the larger z input. Feed all future offsets explicitly to a shared readout. Train-only standardization and validation early stopping are mandatory. Report the readout architecture, label count and learning curve: proposed default is two hidden layers of width 256, ReLU, AdamW at 1e−3, batch 128, at most 10,000 updates, validation every 200 updates with patience 10. Select among learning rates {1e−4, 1e−3} with the same two-trial budget for every readout; freeze the rule before testing.
Primary state baseline L_S is the analytic Bayes predictor E[Y|s] under the evaluation law prior (mean trajectory under squared loss), verified against a strong learned state MLP. Primary oracle L_O is E[Y|s,current law,known transition process]. For deterministic fixed laws this is the exact simulator trajectory. L_J comes from the frozen JEPA history readout. Learned state and learned oracle are diagnostics for readout capacity, not replacements for the analytic primary denominator.
Additional baselines: constant-velocity extrapolation; analytic least-squares acceleration from observed positions; supervised history network using raw frames; shuffled-history JEPA; random encoder; fixed-gravity pretrained encoder; and matched-capacity reconstruction model if budget permits. The position-history estimator uses privileged tracked positions and is an identifiability ceiling, not a fair visual competitor. A vision-only prediction suite is secondary and reports perception errors separately.
# 6. Behavioral abstraction and identifiability measures
Use a common physical target Y: all 3D positions over future offsets 1…h. Primary loss is mean squared Euclidean position error divided by fixed reference length² (L_ref=1 m), averaged over future times, laws, then anchor groups. Do not normalize separately by horizon or condition. Also report endpoint position RMSE in meters, velocity RMSE in m/s, and paired future-difference error. Native JEPA latent losses are not comparable across independently trained encoders and are not used in the primary score.
Abstraction Recovery Score (ARS) = (L_S − L_J) / (L_S − L_O). Gap G=L_S−L_O is mechanism usefulness at matched state. Raw history benefit B=L_S−L_J remains a required endpoint. ARS=0 means state-level performance; ARS=1 reaches the specified oracle; negative values and values above one remain visible. Never clamp the reported score.
Proposed denominator threshold epsilon=0.0025 in normalized position-MSE units (equivalent to a 0.05 m RMS scale when the gap equals this value). ARS is eligible only if G>epsilon and the paired bootstrap lower 95% bound of G exceeds epsilon. Otherwise mark INVALID_GAP and report raw losses and B. If resampled gaps cross zero, do not present finite ratio intervals as reliable; flag the ratio unstable. Pilot must assess this threshold against resolution and scientific relevance before freeze.
Mechanism usefulness U=G is an operational squared-loss benefit, not mutual information. Primary inferability is measured independently by predicting g from history PLUS the exact anchor state, matching the information supplied to the behavioral readout, using an analytic tracked-state fit and a separately trained high-capacity supervised visual estimator. Also report history-only inferability as a secondary diagnostic. With noiseless positions and supplied anchor velocity, two frames may identify gravity exactly; context length alone is therefore not assumed to manipulate inferability cleanly. Include a one-frame pilot negative control and controlled observation noise/occlusion to create low-evidence regimes, then verify inferability empirically. Report gravity MAE, R², and balanced accuracy for discrete laws; compare against the prior-mean/prior-class baseline. For switching worlds evaluate current law and report posterior entropy or calibrated class probabilities. These estimators establish practical recoverability, not proof of Bayes-optimal identifiability.
Optional residual oracle benefit compares context-only and context-plus-law predictors with matched capacity. A small residual benefit can mean history already reveals the law; it must not be interpreted as absence of abstraction. This avoids the conditional-information paradox described in the preview.
Matched-state sensitivity: for each anchor, compare predicted trajectory differences between law pairs to true differences. Report MSE of these differences and agreement of vertical displacement ordering. A state-only model produces the same future for every law variant. Pairing remains intact in every resample.
# 7. Transfer, competing causes, and interventions
Cross-phenomenon protocol: pretrain the history encoder on free-flight clips only. Freeze it. Test representation usefulness on bouncing and pendulum histories with fixed small readout-label budgets of 100, 500, and 2,000 anchor groups. Train/readout splits are independent. Compare (state,z), state-only, random encoder, and true-law oracle within each phenomenon. Keep phenomenon-specific geometry in state and equal for all models. This is frozen-representation transfer with limited adaptation, not zero-shot prediction.
A stricter mechanism-reuse test extracts z from a free-flight source episode and predicts a new target pendulum or bounce episode sharing gravity but having independent state and appearance. Use a target-phenomenon readout trained on source-target pairs under training laws; evaluate held-out states and gravity values 6.5 and 11.5, plus extrapolation at 2 and 18 separately. Shuffling source histories across laws should remove incremental benefit. Evaluate law-consistent source-to-target gains before claiming reuse.
Same-effect/different-cause suite: gravity plus constant external vertical force can create identical acceleration. In condition 1, observation and test future remain observationally equivalent: no model is expected to distinguish causes. In condition 2, provide diagnostic history from a second known mass under the same force, then remove external force at the target anchor. Equal-mass instantaneous states and pre-intervention acceleration are matched; future gravity-only motion differs. The intervention flag and known mass are supplied equally to all readouts, while force and gravity remain hidden except to oracle. New observations identify the causes; without them the task is deliberately unidentifiable.
Also test drag versus gravity: choose different (g,drag) pairs that match acceleration at one velocity, then include histories with changing velocity and evaluate new velocities. Hold all other quantities fixed and validate that the candidate causes actually diverge under the chosen intervention. Results support distinction only for the tested mechanism family.
Representation analyses: per-layer gravity probes with group-held-out splits; nuisance probes; conditional gravity decoding after controlling state; representational similarity within law across state; and state-matched history-vector swaps. A swap should shift predicted futures toward the donor law while preserving anchor state. Report on-manifold consistency checks and no-intervention controls; activation editing is supporting evidence because it may create off-manifold inputs.

# 9. Breaking-frontier design
Prespecified stress axes and five levels: gravity extrapolation distance beyond [4,14] at {0,1,2,4,8} m/s² on each side with gravity constrained positive; RGB noise standard deviation {0,.01,.03,.07,.15} on [0,1]; occluded-frame fraction {0,.1,.25,.5,.75}; observation interval {1,2,3,4,6}/30 s; mean law dwell {fixed,32,16,8,2} frames; and unseen drag coefficient {0,.05,.1,.2,.4} s⁻¹. For gravity, report lower and upper sides separately and identify clipped/unsupported low-gravity levels.
Vary one axis at a time first. A two-axis noise×dwell grid is optional and exploratory. Keep physical future horizon fixed when changing observation sampling; record both frame count and elapsed seconds. Recompute state baseline and non-clairvoyant oracle under each stress distribution.
Define operational success as eligible gap plus ARS lower 95% bound >0.20 and JEPA endpoint RMSE upper 95% bound ≤0.50 m, with thresholds frozen after pilot. A confirmed failure has ARS upper bound <0.20 or endpoint RMSE lower bound >0.50 m. Other cases are uncertain. Report last confirmed success and first confirmed failure as a bracket, never invent a precise crossing; handle non-monotonicity explicitly. Oracle-gap failures are task-ineligible, not model failures.
# 10. Statistical design and decision rules
Independent generation and training are separate variability sources. Pair conditions on corpus seed, evaluation anchor groups, and compatible training-seed IDs. Screening may prioritize conditions but its results cannot become confirmatory evidence. Freeze a small confirmatory contrast family before running fresh seeds: reference versus short context, fixed-law training, short persistence, and the primary transfer versus shuffled-history control.
Primary estimator: compute each run’s mean losses with equal law and anchor weights, average equally across training seeds within corpus, then across corpora. Compute ARS as a ratio of these mean losses, not a mean of row-level ratios. Report both this estimand and seed-level ARS distribution. For OOD suites use their own explicitly specified law prior and oracle.
Uncertainty: 10,000 paired hierarchical bootstrap replicates. Resample training-corpus clusters, then training seeds within corpus; independently resample evaluation-corpus clusters, then matched anchor groups within evaluation corpus. Use the same sampled indices for every model/condition in a contrast; keep every law variant and future frame inside its group. Recompute aggregate losses, G, B, ARS, and contrasts per replicate. Use percentile 2.5/97.5 bounds; report invalid-ratio fraction and mark unstable if any bootstrap denominator is nonpositive or if gap lower bound fails eligibility.
Report corpus-to-corpus variation, within-corpus seed SD, and pooled run-level SD separately. Frames, law variants, and horizon steps are not independent training replicates. With five corpus clusters uncertainty is still approximate: add leave-one-corpus-out sensitivity and state this limitation. Mixed-effects regression with corpus and test group effects may support exploratory trend analysis, but does not replace prespecified paired contrasts.
Pilot precision: estimate cluster variance from at least 2 corpora × 2 seeds and resample/simulate planned designs under ARS contrasts of 0, .10, .20. Choose sample sizes targeting 80% power for .20 or expected 95% CI width ≤.20. Pilot estimates are unstable; publish the sensitivity curve and budget tradeoff. No promise of adequate power follows merely from 15 runs.
Control the four primary confirmatory contrast tests by Holm at familywise alpha .05; report adjusted p-values and effect sizes. Bootstrap contrast intervals are pointwise unless explicitly made simultaneous. Secondary layers/sweeps use Benjamini–Hochberg FDR .05 within named families or descriptive intervals without significance claims. No test-set selection of best layer, horizon, or checkpoint.
Proposed bounded success: reference matched-state ARS lower bound >.20, positive raw history benefit, denominator eligible, no leakage, and a positive prespecified frozen-transfer gain with CI excluding zero after the stated multiplicity procedure. Stronger mechanism-reuse claims additionally require the source-to-target and identifiable cause tests. Values between evidence thresholds are inconclusive; a sufficiently precise CI contained in [−.10,.10] supports a practically null benefit. Negative results require task/optimization validity first.
# 11. Risks and required controls
| Risk | Control | Consequence if unresolved |
| Renderer or filename leaks law | Balance nuisance labels; law-from-single-frame audit; block metadata from loader | Invalidate affected datasets and retrain. |
| Matched-state anchor mismatch | Analytic construction and tolerance tests; identical anchor render | Reject entire group; block evaluation. |
| Baseline too weak | Analytic Bayes baseline plus tuned state MLP | Report baseline bug or approximation error; no ARS claim. |
| Oracle has future random knowledge | Explicit oracle information contract; separate clairvoyant ceiling | Recompute scores and intervals. |
| Encoder collapse or target leakage | Rank/variance monitoring; future-perturbation invariance test | Stop and version objective/data fixes. |
| State shortcuts and local acceleration suffice | Source-target transfer; same-effect/different-cause; supervised history control | Limit claims to reusable acceleration prediction. |
| Rejection changes law distributions | Group-level acceptance; report support and rates | Regenerate on common support. |
| Probe/readout hides good features | Label-budget curves; learned oracle; linear and MLP sensitivity | Interpret failed readout cautiously. |
| Compute confounding | Matched-token, matched-parameter and matched-compute views | Do not attribute effect uniquely to depth or data. |
| Post-selection and missing runs | Frozen registry; failed runs retained; fresh confirmation seeds | Label exploratory; report missingness by condition. |
